## Retention decision

There is no universal legal maximum that permits holding all captures forever. Keep each category only while reasonably necessary for its disclosed purpose. An active account can support retaining the personal twin for continuing try-on; it does not by itself justify indefinite retention of every raw photo or video. Honor applicable deletion rights and stricter deadlines. Do not treat new legal text as consent to reuse earlier captures for model training.

Before connecting uploads, select the actual inactivity threshold, raw-capture retention rationale, backup expiration and deletion execution periods; connect the corresponding deletion jobs and access controls. Disclose recipients and actual hosting/logging practices. Record the accepted document version and timestamp server-side, and assess whether separate biometric consent is required by the chosen reconstruction pipeline. Review all applicable US state rules; the notes here are not a fifty-state clearance.

A full-body photo is not automatically a statutory biometric identifier. The analysis changes if processing extracts regulated face/hand geometry or biometric identification data. For example, Illinois BIPA can require a public retention/destruction policy and informed written release before collection, with destruction when the original purpose is satisfied or within three years of last interaction, whichever is earlier. The official statute search result was available, but direct full-text retrieval was blocked during this review; counsel should verify the current statute and pipeline before launch. No claim is made that BIPA applies to the current local preview.
